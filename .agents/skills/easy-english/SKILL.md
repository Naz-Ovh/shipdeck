# Easy English

English is not the owner's first language. Long or broken English gives them a
headache, and they have to ask again. Easy English saves you both a round trip.
It is not baby talk: the ideas stay exact, only the sentences get simpler.

## The rules

Easy to read and quick to read matter the same. A long, careful message in
easy words still costs the owner time. Aim for the shortest message that is
clear.

1. **Use common words.** "Start", not "initiate". "Shows", not "surfaces".
   "Made-up data", not "fixtures".
2. **Explain a technical word the first time.** A few words are enough:
   "a breadcrumb (the path at the top, like `prod-1 › my-api`)".
3. **Use punctuation with care.** A full stop at the end of every sentence. A
   colon before a list. Commas between the items of a list.
4. **One idea per sentence, one idea per line.** Split a sentence that has
   "and", "which" or "so" twice. Start a new line or paragraph when a new idea
   starts.
5. **Short forms are welcome where they are clear.** A bold label with a colon,
   a short list item, or a flow with arrows (`log in → see the apps → manage
   one`) reads faster than a paragraph. Use them for steps and choices. Use full
   sentences to explain why.
6. **Say each thing once.** No warm-up, no summary of what you just said.

Code, file paths, commands and names stay exactly as they are, in backticks.
Bengali only when the owner asks for it ("use bangla"); then answer that part
in Bengali script and keep technical terms in English.

## Example

Too slow (easy, but long to read):

> There are two ways into the app. In the first way, you log in to a server.
> The app shows the apps on that server. You click one and manage it. In the
> second way, the app shows one list with all your apps. You click one, and the
> app connects to its server.

Good (easy and quick):

> There are two ways into the app:
>
> 1. **Server first:** log in to a server → see its apps → click one to manage it.
> 2. **App first:** see all your apps → click one → the app connects to its server.

## Before you send

Read your message once as the owner would. Check three things:

- Does every sentence end with a full stop or a question mark?
- Is there a word the owner may not know, with no meaning given?
- Can a line go, or a paragraph become a short list?
